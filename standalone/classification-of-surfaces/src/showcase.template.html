Three pictures of one thing

A repeating pattern has symmetries: turns, mirrors, and two subtler kinds. Its signature, the line you type in the toy, lists them. The toy’s first picture is a pattern with exactly those symmetries. Fold that pattern up, tile onto tile, until every tile lies on one, and you hold a surface with a few special points on it: the pattern’s orbifold, the second picture. The signature is also the list of what is on that surface. p. 75

Each tile carries the same print, a family of commas. A comma has a hand and no symmetry of its own, so the pattern has exactly the signature’s. Where a mirror or a miracle carries a tile, the print arrives mirrored, and nothing else tells such a tile from the others: a second colour for them would take the mirrors away again.

One tile is outlined. What happens to its edges gives the surface. A mirror edge (red) stays an edge: the rim of a hole. Two edges of the same colour, with the same number of arrowheads, are glued along their arrows. Corners that meet at a point make a cone point, or a corner of the rim if they meet on a mirror.

The Magic Theorem

Everything in a signature has a price, the last column above. Add it up. p. 29

  • Exactly $2: a pattern on the plane. That leaves 17 signatures. p. 30
  • Under $2: a pattern on a sphere, and the change is 2 over the number of symmetries. p. 53
  • Over $2: a pattern on the hyperbolic plane. Every such signature has one. p. 237

The price is what each thing takes off the surface’s Euler characteristic: a sphere has 2, a handle takes 2, a hole or a crosscap 1. A cone point of order \(n\) is shared by \(n\) tiles, so it counts as \(\frac{1}{n}\) of a point and takes \(\frac{n-1}{n}\).

Typing a signature

The keys o, 2 to 9, ∗ and x add a character after the cursor. Delete takes one away, all the way to an empty line: a bare sphere. The arrows move the cursor, and with a number chosen − and + step it. The chips under the signature do the same for a finger.

Where a number stands matters: before the star it is a turn (blue), after a star a crossing of that many mirrors (red). So 4∗ and ∗4 are different things. What does not matter: the order of the blue numbers, which number of a star comes first, reading every star backwards, and the order of the stars. Type any of these and the page shows the usual spelling. p. 231

Under $2 four kinds of signature have no pattern: N, MN, ∗N and ∗MN, with M and N different. On a sphere a turn about a point is a turn about the opposite point too, and mirrors through a point cross again opposite, so such things come in equal pairs. The page says which rule a signature breaks and offers the nearest ones that work. p. 53

The hyperbolic plane on a flat screen

The disc is Poincaré’s picture of it. Angles are true; straight lines show as arcs that meet the rim squarely; and the rim is infinitely far away, so the tiles crowding toward it are all the size of the one in the middle. Drag any of them to the middle to see. p. 221
