## Findings, worst first

1. **In half the family there are no letters, only a fret.** In the nine groups with mirrors (\*x, 22\*, \*2222,
   2\*22, 3\*3, \*333, 4\*2, \*442, \*632) the F is built along the tile's sides, which are mirrors, so every stem and
   every arm lies 0.068 from its own reflection, with the ink of the mirror line drawn in that channel. F and its
   reflection fuse into one symmetric sign (E, T, H, Ш, Y), and the field reads as a Greek key / basket weave in
   which the eye finds the letter only inside the highlighted tile. That is exactly the wrong way round: the letter is
   there to show that a mirror reverses the hand, and across a mirror is where it stops being a letter. Worst:
   \*632 (a hexagonal maze with confetti), \*333 and 3\*3 (chevrons and Y's), 4\*2 and \*442 (E's and crosses).
2. **\*632's own F is unreadable.** Its top is at the right-angle corner and its foot in the 6-fold tip, i.e. it
   stands on its head on the page, the hypotenuse cuts both arms, and a square dot sits in its bay: in the highlighted
   tile it reads as a "q" or a maze cell, not an F (`v1-back.png`, `crit/v1-zoomB.png`).
3. **Too dark where it hurts most.** 0.60 in 4\*2 and \*442, 0.58 in \*632, 0.57 in 22\*: the white is the minority
   and becomes the figure (white crosses and stars on a dark ground). The concept asks for 42 to 50 %.
4. **Rows of F's on their sides** (\*x three, 22\* and 2\*22 two per tile): a lying F of 0.70 x 0.36 reads as a comb,
   a chair or castle battlements ("ш ш ш"), and identical F's give the row a period of a third (a half) of the tile
   that the group does not have; only the full stop at the row's end breaks it. On the Möbius band the row is a ring
   of gear teeth (`v1-fold-t06.png`, `v1-fold-t1.png`).
5. **\*2222 lies on its side.** The F lies on its stem, so the four copies at each mirror corner make T's and crosses.
   The one picture every crystallographer knows (four upright F's around a mirror corner: F, its mirror, upside down,
   turned) is missed in the very group it was drawn for.
6. **Speckle.** The full stops that are put into bare patches rather than at an F's foot (2222, 333, 442, \*632) are
   squares 0.17 to 0.25 wide floating free of their letter; in \*632 they are confetti on the fret. The chip of
   \*333 and 3\*3 is a wedge that reads as a broken-off piece.
7. **In the fold the letter survives only on flat pieces.** On the thin tubes (o, xx, \*x, \*\*) the stem runs
   along the tube and the arms wrap round it: the torus and the cylinder show blobs, the Klein bottle bands, the
   Möbius band teeth. A whole F is legible in 3D only on \*\*'s cylinder at t = 0.6 (front) and on the flat
   kaleidoscope triangles at t = 1 (\*2222, \*333, \*442, sand side). Partly the app's (the tube's circumference is
   the tile's short side), but a lying F makes it worse. Which side is up is always clear (teal vs sand).
8. **The sand side is faint in 3D.** Flat (`v1-back.png`) the sand F reads; on the closed shapes, which show mostly
   the back of the paper, the marks are low-contrast blobs. The design cannot change the colours; it can make the
   marks bigger and calmer, which helps.
9. **Thirty knobs and a discrete search.** Which corner, which extra (foot, patch, chip, long, bold) a group gets
   can jump with a small change of the lattice. A maintenance risk, not a picture problem.

## What is good and must be kept

- o, xx, 22x, 442 and \*\*: a big upright F (0.9 to 1.4 tile sizes) with its full stop, filling the tile; the copies
  turn and flip visibly; 22x shows rotated and glided F's side by side, 442 a pinwheel of F's. This is the brief.
- The numbers: dark within 0.40 to 0.62, bare at most 0.447, solid at most 0.34, no overlap, fast, few points.
- Re-cut (xx, 22x): the pieces carry the F in register; the F reassembles in the 3D pane. Tile step: the cards
  carry the print. Phone: o and 22x read; \*632 is a grey maze at that size.

## What the owner would say

"In o, xx and 22x yes, this is the comma, and it fills the surface. In the mirror groups I see a carpet, not
letters." The design does not ship as it is: the mirror groups, a good half of the page, need their letters back.

## After the improvement (NOTES.md, "After the critique", has the changes and the numbers)

| 2 \*632's F unreadable | partly: the dot in its bay is gone and the F is clean, but it still stands on its head on the page (no normal F in that chamber reaches the 6-fold tip any other way) |

Verdict after the improvement: better than the delivered version in four groups (\*2222, \*442, \*632, 333/442's
dots) and equal elsewhere, every number within the limits (bare at most 0.442), but it still does not give the owner
letters in the mirror groups. A strong pattern for the groups without mirrors; as the family's one print, not yet.
