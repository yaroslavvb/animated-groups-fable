# Two features, two handles (Demo 2a)

Interactive Fenchel–Nielsen explorer across all five Conway–Thurston feature kinds (`cone n`, composite `22`, mirrors `∗`/`∗3`, crosscap `×`, and handle halves `o⁺`/`o⁻`) in seven families: `3333`, `2223`, `333∗`, `333∗3`, `333×`, `o33` (hyperbolic), and `2222` (Euclidean). Tap any two features on the 3D surface (or their symbols in the header) to switch the active pair, tree, and isolating cut while holding every cone point, geodesic cuff, mirror corner, and rigid comma stationary in place (`1e-9` precision) with the Pattern view uncentered by default (Pattern `Reset` centers on the active pair).

Incorporates Vladimir Bulatov's and Chaim Goodman-Strauss's email feedback after seeing the first four-cone version: generalizing pair separation and twist from cone pairs to all five feature kinds (`∗`, `∗3`, `×`, `½ o`) and adding the `o` handle fault-line shift parameter (`tau_o`, shifting `o⁻` against `o⁺` along the blue handle geodesic `gamma_o`), cuff lengths, and exact recursive re-cutting across any pair.

## More pairs behind a plus, and every other orbifold shown still (Thu 26 Nov 2026, week 48)

His ask, 14:28, from the same Claude session in `animated-groups-fable` (`40b381a4`), after the print below was live:

> *"and user interface to allow modifying more orbifolds, more than just 2 pairs ones like 2222. Perhaps a plus button I can click to select another pair for modification. Also support other orbifolds which don't have these parameters. In that case, the pair controls are just disabled."*

- **Five or six cone points** (`22222`, `22233`, `223344`, `234567`: any orders 2–9, typed in the field). The cone points are taken two by two in the order typed: `22 33 44` is three pairs, `22 23 3` two pairs and one cone point left over. Every pair is parted from the rest by an isolating cut of its own, with a Length and a Twist of its own, and those are all the numbers of shape the orbifold has (4 for five cone points, 6 for six). What is left in the middle is a pair of pants.
- **The plus.** The feature symbols stand pair by pair, with a gap between pairs. The first pair is picked; the others are gray, and a `+` after them picks the next: its symbols light, the sliders show its numbers, its tree and cut are drawn heavy (the pairs picked before keep theirs, lighter), and its loop is the heavy one on the surface. A tap on a symbol, or on a feature of the surface, picks that feature's pair. The `+` goes when every pair is in play. Nothing moves on the pattern when a pair is picked.
- **The sliders** move the picked pair's Length and Twist and no other number. The picked pair is what stays on the screen: under its Twist its two cone points do not move at all, under its Length its middle stays. `Reset` lays the picked pair level in the middle.
- **Opens as classified.** The default numbers are the Classification of Surfaces page's shape (its polygon with the cone points at its corners: each cut is the line square to the two sides next to the pair's own; every Twist 0), and there the pattern is that page's, lying as it lies there, with its commas. The print follows the rule of the section below, with a joint for every cut: each comma is tied whole to a cone point of its block, or to the middle piece (with six cone points both commas lie in the middle piece), so no Twist of any pair breaks a comma or runs one into another. Of the signatures tried only `99999` (twelve commas a tile) and `29292` have commas larger than their blocks: drawn in to 96 % once a Twist leaves 0.
- **The address** carries every pair: `#k=223344&p=2&n=2&l=2.932,3.778,3.984&tw=0,0.33,0` (`p` the picked pair, `n` how many are in play, then a Length and a Twist a pair). A link copied at the defaults opens as classified.
- **Every other orbifold** that has a pattern is shown as the Classification page shows it (its tile, its print, its surface), with the pair controls off: the feature symbols gray, the sliders pale and out of reach of pointer and keyboard, and a line under them that says why. Rigid: `237`, `∗632`, `532`. Or with numbers of shape that are not built here: `∗2222` has 1, `2∗33` 1, `oo` 6, `2222222` 8 (`custom.js` `movesOf` counts them: 3 for each handle or crosscap beyond a sphere, 2 a cone point, 1 a corner).
- **What the field still turns away:** text that is no signature, and the four shapes no pattern folds up to (one cone point alone, two of different orders, a mirror with one corner alone, a mirror's two corners of different orders). The note under the field says which, and offers the nearest that have a pattern. Three characters of `2379` on the way are now an orbifold of their own (`237`, shown still) where the note used to say "three features".
- **How.** `src/multi.js` lays the pair of pants down as two right-angled hexagons and builds every turn from mirrors (the seams of the hexagon, and for each pair two lines square to its cut half a Length apart), so the turns multiply to 1 by construction, whatever the numbers. The tile is the set of points nearer to a point of the middle piece than to any copy of it (convex at every Length and Twist, which the pattern pane's walk needs), found with the turns as spinors and certified by its area: only finitely many copies are looked at, so the polygon could be too large, never too small, and the search goes on until it has the area Gauss–Bonnet gives the signature.
- **Three faults found by sweeping random shapes, each fixed at its root:**
  - *A pair on the wrong side of its cut.* The point of the middle piece was picked by coordinates (a quarter along cuff 1, a third of the way to cuff 2). With cuff 3 long that cuff runs 0.6 from cuff 1 and the point lay beyond it, in pair 3's own piece; the code that puts pair 3 "on the side away from the middle" then put it on the wrong one, and the turns made a group that is no pattern at all (1 shape in 4,800). The point is now the heart of the hexagon (the mean of its corners), inside whatever the lengths.
  - *The same tile listed again and again.* The pattern was first handed to the pane in the picked pair's frame, as the four-feature families are. With a short cut and cone points of high order a pair stands 5 from the middle piece: the tile lay that far out, and the motions to its neighbours were matrices with entries of `1e9`. The pane's walk then met the same tile without knowing it (4,200 tiles listed for 550, at about 1 shape in 80). The pattern is now handed over in the frame of its tile, with the motions as the spinors they were found as, and a `pose` that says where the picked pair lies; `pattern-ext.js` `set` takes a `carry` (the change of pose) so that the pair stays put while its sliders move.
  - *An edge parted in two.* A copy that already owned an edge came back as another product, its line a hair off, and cut its own edge at a corner that is nowhere. A copy that owns an edge cuts nothing more.
- **Limits.**
  - The pairs are the ones the order typed gives: `223344` pairs the 2s, the 3s and the 4s; `232434` would pair 2 with 3, 2 with 4, 3 with 4. Re-pairing by tapping two features (the four-feature families' re-cut) is not built for these.
  - Seven or more cone points, and anything with a mirror, a crosscap or a handle beyond four features, are shown still: their cuts are not built.
  - A Length runs from 0.8 to 5.5 on the slider (a link may say 0.7 to 6). Outside that the tile search was not failure-free (13 of 3,000 shapes with Lengths from 0.4 to 7).
  - Length carries the commas rigid, as on the four-feature families: at a Length far from the default they can come to overlap, and with a short cut and high orders the picked pair's piece is large and bare.
  - No moments (`Follow the tree` and the rest) for these: the row is hidden.

## A Twist breaks no comma: the print rides in blocks (Thu 26 Nov 2026, week 48)

His asks, about 13:28 and 13:35, on seeing the print below (each comma cut along the isolating cut, its halves sliding apart under Twist), from the same Claude session in `animated-groups-fable` (`40b381a4`):

1. *"Can you make sure that this is changed so that there are less breaks in pattern. For instance in …two-features-two-handles#k=2222&m=0123&l=1&tw=0.0067, if I apply the twist, it breaks comma. Find out what is done in …twist-of-2222#l=1&t=0.4281&c=0.5 to avoid breaks in commas. On that page, all the sharing I can apply doesn't actually change the pattern. The commas are all whole. If it's possible to make generalizable, apply this to other orbifolds as well."*
2. *"I think one of the issues that for 2222, we can draw on the original orbifold, and then twist parameter just affects how the copies of that pattern stack up together. For the other instances, I'm currently drawing, I think, on a 2D plane. I'm going to find a way to draw the corresponding orbifold (these two commas) so that there are no breaks after applying symmetry."*

- **What the Twist of 2222 page does** ([its README](../2026-11-24-twist-of-2222/README.md), fourth version, the same day). Its print belongs to the paper: the dominoes along a row of the picked pair make a block, and a twist slides whole blocks along the rows of the other pair's centres. The print's joint is that row (the far seal), where no comma lies, not the loop drawn between the two rows.
- **What carries over to every orbifold.** A Twist slides the two sides of the isolating cut along it, and that slide carries every curve that keeps its distance from the cut along itself (on the plane a parallel line: the far row is one). So the print may be parted along any such curve instead of the cut. `paisley.js` `jointOf` looks for one that runs through no comma: an offset from the cut, between the two limits where the joints of neighbouring lifts would meet or a cone point be passed (a pair of half-turn centres lies ON its cut, and the cut itself is then the joint, a seal). With such a joint the orbifold falls into pieces that no Twist changes, the commas are drawn whole on them, and a Twist only changes how the pieces are stacked. That is his second message, made exact.
- **Where it holds as it stands.** `3333` (joint 0.62 from the cut, of 0.66 possible), `2222` (0.47 of 0.5: the far row), `2223` (the cut itself, a seal through its two far half-turn centres), `o33` (0.66 of 0.84), and of the typed ones tried `2345`, `9999`, `2229`, `2245`, `2227`, `3456`, `957∗`, `o27`, `o22`: every comma at full size, whole at every Twist.
- **Where a comma is larger than any block.** `333∗`, `333∗3` and `333×` have one comma to a tile, and it fills the tile: every curve a Twist can slide along runs through it. It is tied whole to the block its middle is in, and drawn in toward its cone point until the joints miss it: to 87 %, 86 % and 88 %. At the family's own Twist it is that page's comma, at full size; it is drawn in over the first fortieth of a turn of Twist away from there (`scaleAt`, `TURN`), and back again on return. So nothing breaks and nothing overlaps, at the price of a comma that is a little smaller while twisted. (Typed `234∗5`: 94 %; `957×`: 98 %.)
- **A pick.** Nothing moves, as before. The commas are tied anew to the blocks of the new pair's cut (`retie`, kept in the page's state as `state.print`), so a Twist along that cut slides them whole too. On `3333` and `2223` every one of the six pairs has a joint that misses that page's commas at full size; on `2222` four do, and the two diagonal pairs draw the commas in to 85 % (the Twist of 2222 page redraws its print there).
- **A link.** A link carries no print. One to another pair than the family's own gets the print that the shortest chain of picks naming its four features leaves on the default pattern (`handles.js` `linkedPrint`: all 24 namings on `3333` and `2223`, the 6 the page ever writes on the others), and its numbers carry it from there.
- **Limits.** Length (and the cuff sliders) still carry each comma rigid with its cone point: the blocks change shape under them, and commas can come to overlap or part (the Twist of 2222 page re-lays its print for every length; not done here). A print reached by picks after a Twist is the session's own: its link opens on the print described above, which can differ. The joint is not drawn.

- **Not the same as that page, on purpose:** this page draws its tree and its isolating cut, not that page's outlined tile with its gluing arrows nor its faint tile edges; a mark keeps this page's turn (along the tree) and the picked pair's larger size; the surface keeps this page's camera.
- **Limits.** See the section above. A typed signature with a cuff does not get that page's picture (above).

One real change beyond orders: with two half-turn points on the new cut (`o22`, picking the handle's two sides) the cone points lie ON the cut, so the handle now says which side is which (`recutO33`, mixed to split).
