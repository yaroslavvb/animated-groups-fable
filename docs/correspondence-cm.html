Diagram key

Crystallographic generator symbols: visual index

Projected along the clock axis, these are the actual symbols used on the static 2D diagrams. Open arrows mark primitive translations in the picture; the crystallographic filled core shows the 2-, 3-, 4-, or 6-fold spatial order. For a forward skip τ, the screw subscript is m ≡ nτ (mod n) for a positive 1/n-turn and m ≡ −nτ (mod n) for the opposite turn, with 0 ≤ m < n. Screw tails prolong the polygon edges, as in the Tables; a mirrored pinwheel records the opposite spatial turn sense, not backward time.

Mirror and glide planes (seen edge-on)

  • Mirror plane (m)Solid line; reflection with no glide component.
  • In-plane axial glideDashed line with a half arrow; reflection plus a half-cell step, the arrow giving the direction of that step.
  • Clock-axis glide (c)Dotted line; reflection plus a half-period step perpendicular to the picture.
  • Diagonal n-glideDash–dot line; half-steps in the picture and along the clock axis.
  • Quarter-diagonal d-glideArrowed dash–dot line; arrows select the sign of the quarter-period component.

Notation

Only cyclic colour permutations are considered here. That restriction is what makes the correspondence exact: a colouring whose permutations form a cyclic group is the same object as a polar crystal, the colour cycle being the height along the polar axis. Each row is therefore one colouring and one polar space group, named both ways.

Names on the left are the coloured-orbifold signatures of The Symmetries of Things ↗ (Conway, Burgiel and Goodman-Strauss); each row links to the book page its short form is read from. Names on the right are the standard crystallographic ones — the Hermann–Mauguin symbol and its number in the International Tables for Crystallography, Volume A ↗, with each row also linking to its entry in the Bilbao Crystallographic Server ↗ and to a real mineral bearing the group.
