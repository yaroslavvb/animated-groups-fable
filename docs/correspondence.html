How the orbifold generator inclusions and still-frame selector work

All 68 groups are placed on the shared base translation lattice T = ⟨t₁, t₂⟩ with origin (0, 0) so that every one of the 123 immediate covering edges adds a single symmetry generator going downward: rotations R₂ (180°), R₃ (120°), R₄ (90°), R₆ (60°); mirrors ∗ₓ (horizontal), ∗_d (diagonal), ∗_d′ (anti-diagonal); glides ×ᵧ (vertical) and ×ₓ′ (offset horizontal); or centred sublattice translations t(0,½), t(½,½), t(⅔,⅓). A superscript ^(+1/N) indicates that the generator also advances the cyclic colour/clock phase by 1/N of a period.

Among the 17 monochrome orbifold groups, fixing a single origin and unit lattice realises 27 direct generator inclusions simultaneously; the remaining 4 abstract same-lattice index-2 pairs (22∗ ⊂ ∗2222, ×× ⊂ 22×, 4∗2 ⊂ ∗442, and the second diagonal mirror into ∗333) require shifting the origin by a quarter-period or rotating the mirror axis, because in a fixed coordinate group two distinct index-2 subgroups on the same lattice generate a unique order-4 supergroup (⟨R₂, ∗ₓ⟩ = ∗2222 and ⟨R₄, ∗_d⟩ = ∗442).

In a single frozen frame (τ = 0), only the colour-fixing subgroup K = ker χ is visible; when the animation is rolling, forgetting the clock projects the group to the spatial orbifold group Γ. In 45 of the 68 groups (K ≇ Γ), the projected orbifold symmetry is absent from individual frames and only appears when the animation rolls — including 8 pure translation groups (K = ◦) whose frozen frames have no point or mirror symmetry at all. In another 6 centred groups, each frame still has a copy of Γ on a coarser sublattice, while the remaining 17 one-colour groups have their full symmetry in every frame.

Notation

Only cyclic colour permutations are considered here. That restriction is what makes the correspondence exact: a colouring whose permutations form a cyclic group is the same object as a polar crystal, the colour cycle being the height along the polar axis. Each row is therefore one colouring and one polar space group, named both ways.

Names on the left are the coloured-orbifold signatures of The Symmetries of Things ↗ (Conway, Burgiel and Goodman-Strauss); each row links to the book page its short form is read from. Names on the right are the standard crystallographic ones — the Hermann–Mauguin symbol and its number in the International Tables for Crystallography, Volume A ↗, with each row also linking to its entry in the Bilbao Crystallographic Server ↗ and to a real mineral bearing the group.

Crystallographic generator symbols: visual index

Projected along the clock axis, these are the actual symbols used on the static 2D diagrams. Open arrows mark primitive translations in the picture; the crystallographic filled core shows the 2-, 3-, 4-, or 6-fold spatial order. For a forward skip τ, the screw subscript is m ≡ nτ (mod n) for a positive 1/n-turn and m ≡ −nτ (mod n) for the opposite turn, with 0 ≤ m < n. Screw tails prolong the polygon edges, as in the Tables; a mirrored pinwheel records the opposite spatial turn sense, not backward time.
