THE RULE WE ARE LOOKING FOR

Move forward in time.
Move back in space.

S simulate(q, T/3) = q

q is two chemical fields. S is the array permutation called slide. If the evolved, slid frame matches the starting frame, three repetitions return to the same picture after one period.

This is the simplest algorithm in §16 of “Symmetric animation — simplified”. It imposes a turn with no time delay and a slide paired with a third of a period. The published film also has mirrors; these two operations alone do not specify its full symmetry group.

What counts as success?

The stopping test measures one discrete symmetry residual. A uniform equilibrium also passes it. Check that the answer varies in both space and time, that a full unprojected period closes, and that a smaller time step gives the same result. The verification accompanying this tutorial does these checks and compares a phase-aligned answer with the published Float32 frame.

The free period and the averaging filter work well for this example. They are a numerical search method, without a general guarantee of convergence or a proof for the continuous PDE. The initial value 6.355 is a period guess, not a measured result; the solver refines it.
