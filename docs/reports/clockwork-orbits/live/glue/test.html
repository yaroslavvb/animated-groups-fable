A fundamental domain that remembers

Delayed glue on the 333 orbifold of g227 — standalone test page for live/glue/.

A simulator like SymSim draws a wallpaper pattern by integrating one fundamental domain and gluing its edges to copies of itself. The catalog's clockwork convention is q(Mx + v, t + τgT) = q(x, t). Read that node-wise, with y = gx:

q(y, t) = q(g⁻¹y, t − τgT)

The neighbour across a glued edge is the representative's own past. Gluing with a lag turns the reaction–diffusion equation on the fundamental domain into a delay-differential equation whose only extra parameter is the period T — the delay. Nothing else changes: the same six-neighbour triangular Laplacian, the same Brusselator, the same RK4. Give the domain a history buffer and the clockwork orbit is no longer a saddle to be solved for; it is an attractor you can watch assemble.

What the page just did

  • White noise of rms 0.10 about the uniform equilibrium (1, 2.3) on the 146 representatives, from a seeded generator — the same run every time.
  • RK4 on the delayed system, dt = T/336 so that T/3 is an exact number of steps, with 4-point Lagrange interpolation of the history for the midpoint stages.
  • The delay starts at the Hopf period 6.355088 = 2π/Im λ and is updated once per period by the phase-shift feedback T′ = T + ⟨q(t−T) − q(t), dq/dt⟩ / |dq/dt|².
  • It stops when the delay changes by less than 1e−5 twice running, and then — only then — takes two Newton steps on the twisted shooting equation.

Self-test

The reduced right-hand side (146 nodes, six neighbours gathered through the glue) against the full-torus right-hand side of live/newton/core.mjs (1296 nodes) applied to the expanded field. These must agree to the last bit; if they do not, the glue tables are wrong.
