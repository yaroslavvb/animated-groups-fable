Subgroups, Cosets & Perfect Colourings: D₃ and 442 (p4)

Why do left cosets gS always give a perfect colour symmetry, while S-orbits (right cosets Sg) break whenever S is non-normal? Explore every subgroup of the triangle group D₃ (order 6), then watch the exact same mechanism expand into the infinite planar rotation group 442 (p4) through Gaussian integer ideals and all 135 subgroups up to index 10.

1. The Two-Sentence Proof: Left Cosets vs. Orbits

Suppose symmetry group G acts rigidly on tiles from the left (x ↦ gx), so a single base tile carries label e and every other tile carries a unique label g ∈ G. Pick any subgroup S ≤ G of index k = [G : S]:

Partition of G Definition Action of h ∈ G from the left When does h preserve the colours?
Left cosets gS x ~ y ⇔ x⁻¹y ∈ S h · (gS) = (hg)S Always (all h ∈ G) → every gS colouring is perfect!
S-orbits / Right cosets Sg x ~ y ⇔ y ∈ Sx h · (Sg) = (hSh⁻¹)(hg) Only when hSh⁻¹ = S, i.e. h ∈ N_G(S) (breaks outside normaliser unless S ◁ G).
Why non-normal subgroups matter: In D₃, the 3 conjugates ⟨b⟩, ⟨ab⟩, ⟨a²b⟩ produce 3 distinct left-coset colourings (Table 2's C8, C7, C6 — rotated copies of one pattern) and 1 orbit class. In 442, at index k = 4, a non-normal C₄ placement has 4 left cosets that stay perfect under every 90° turn and translation, whereas its C₄-orbits collapse to another conjugate's orbits as soon as you translate by tₓ.

2. Resolving the Notation: Bridges 2001 vs. Spacetime Groups Catalogue

Two complementary traditions use the letter H for two different groups:

Role in a k-colouring P De Las Peñas (Bridges 2001) This visualizer / Spacetime Groups Group-theoretic meaning
Subgroup permuting all colours of P H H_P Full symmetry group of P iff H_P = G (perfect colouring).
Subgroup fixing every colour individually K K_P = core_{H_P}(S) Normal kernel ⋂_{h ∈ H_P} hSh⁻¹ of the permutation action on colours.
Stabiliser of colour #1 J₁ (often H when H = G) S (or H in G/H[n]) Index-k subgroup whose cosets give the k colours when P is transitive.
Induced permutation group on k colours H / K ↪ S_k Q = G / K_P Quotient acting faithfully on the k colours (Chaim / Macdonald colour group).
Action handedness on paper vs. screen Paper writes right-cosets Hg (right action) Screen applies rigid motions x ↦ gx (left action) Inversion g ↔ g⁻¹ maps left cosets gS onto right cosets Sg⁻¹; both classifications are isomorphic.
