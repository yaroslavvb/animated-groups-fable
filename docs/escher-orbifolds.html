1. Hyperbolic Escher works (ℍ2, cost > 2)

Every point inside the Poincaré disk lies in ℍ2, where hyperbolic lines appear as circular arcs perpendicular to the rim. Below are exact Minkowski-hyperboloid reconstructions of the four Circle Limit groups and their geometric precursor.

Triangles invert across hyperbolic geodesics right to the rim.

Why no 2-colour swap exists Dark fish have 60° noses and 90° tails; light fish have 90° noses and 60° tails. Along each backbone line, traffic flips direction and colour every two fish lengths — a flaw Escher fixed in Circle Limit III.

Circle Limit II — 3-Coloured Four-Armed Crosses

Unique ℤ3 homomorphism In 3*4 = ⟨ R, m | R3 = m2 = (m R m R−1)4 = 1⟩, mapping R ↦ (0 1 2) forces every mirror m ↦ id. Thus all mirrors keep colours fixed, and the colour-preserving kernel is the equilateral 45° mirror triangle *444.

Circle Limit III — Four-Coloured Schools of Fish

1959 · Cat. 434

Four fish meet at right fin tips (4, disk center), three at left fin tips (3), and three head-to-tail where white backbones cross (3). Because 90° ≠ 120°, the fish are chiral and have zero mirrors.

Circle Limit IV (Heaven and Hell) — Symmetry-Breaking Ladder

July 1960 · Cat. 436

The silhouettes of angels and devils sit in a hyperbolic square tiling of 4*3, where bilateral mirrors bound 60°-cornered squares around 4-fold wingtip centers. Escher's interior shading then breaks that symmetry in stages.

Stage 1 — Outlines / uniform detail: 4*3 ([4+, 6], cost = 25/12, χ = -1/12, index 2 in [6,4]). Every angel and devil carries full identical weight, preserving both mirrors and 4-fold wingtip centers.
Stage 2 — 2-fold dominance (Dunham Fig. 7): 2*33 (cmm3,3, cost = 13/6, χ = -1/6, index 2 in 4*3). Opposite square quadrants alternate angel-dominant vs. devil-dominant, halving the 4-fold centers to 2-fold.
Stage 3 — Periodic mirror-only dominance (Dunham Fig. 5): *3333 (cost = 7/3, χ = -1/3, index 4 in 4*3). Often misattributed to the finished print: every wingtip rotation disappears, leaving four *3 corners per square.
Stage 4 — Actual finished woodcut: finite *3 (D3, order 6). Escher alternated six global wedges around the center (three devil-dominant, three angel-dominant), breaking all hyperbolic translations—ignoring his bottom MCE VII-'60 signature.

3. Hyperbolic extensions beyond Escher (Dunham)

Beginning in the late 1970s, Douglas Dunham used hyperbolic replication algorithms to push Escher's Euclidean motifs past the cost = 2 barrier into infinitely many hyperbolic triangle families.

Switch between the Hurwitz minimal-area group 732 and 5-colour 552.

Chiral Hyperbolic Extensions: 732 & 552

Dunham (1986–2002)
732 (Hurwitz group, χ = -1/42, cost = 85/42): The smallest possible positive excess among all chiral hyperbolic orbifolds. Seven butterfly copies meet around each 2π/7 center, three at wing-junctions, and two at body midpoints. Quotienting by its index-168 Klein-quartic kernel K tiles a genus-3 surface with 168 Escher butterflies; the 7-colouring shown uses the exact Fano-plane homomorphism 732 → PSL(2,7) ⊂ S7 (index-7 stabilizer 2223).
552 ([5,5]+ fish, χ = -1/10, cost = 21/10): Hyperbolic analogue of Escher's 4-colour Euclidean Drawing #20 (442). Five fish meet at both heads and tails around 5-fold gyrations, forcing a 5-colour cyclic permutation because 5 is prime.
