FIRST: IS IT POSSIBLE?

Numerical examples for all six symmetries.

Gray–Scott is equivariant under square-lattice rotations and translations. Every group here advances time; none reverses it. Their constraints are therefore compatible with the equations. Two computed branches now provide nonuniform periodic examples for all six requested time characters. Their translated or reflected copies are checked separately. Glider-shaped solutions remain an open search target.

GroupTime actionMost plausible search targetAssessment

A single rigidly drifting glider lattice cannot have a quarter-turn symmetry: its drift velocity would have to equal its own 90° rotation. Look instead for rotating packets, coordinated gliders, or oscillating worms. The mirror-plus-time-shift idea is also compatible with these equations, but mirrors belong to other wallpaper families; 442 itself has rotations only.

WHAT HAS BEEN FOUND?

Standing and rotating concentration waves

The atlas contains 77 verified entries at 12 physical parameter sets, covering all six groups. Standing waves have a half-period shift; rotating waves have a quarter-period shift. Additional spatial modes give multiple patterns at some identical parameter sets. Reflected or translated counterparts are separately checked, and remain related examples of the same branch. The reference solutions use F = 0.00408998727, k = 0.02, Dᵤ = 0.16, Dᵥ = 0.08 and a square of side 256. They were solved on 24² and 48² grids. Across all delivered 48² fields, the largest independently refined return error is 7.97 × 10⁻⁹ RMS, and the largest generator-phase error is 1.81 × 10⁻⁸. These are finite-grid numerical solutions. The 24²-to-48² period change reaches 2.34% at the most distant parameter point, so small shooting errors must not be confused with continuum accuracy.

These are smooth chemical waves with additional symmetries, rather than the gliders in the reference images. The g95 standing wave fails g96’s quarter-shift test, so the two characters cannot be interchanged. The parameter catalog, thumbnails and numerical checks are precomputed. THE EQUATIONS

Local chemistry, global timing.

∂u/∂t = Dᵤ Δu − uv² + F(1 − u)
∂v/∂t = Dᵥ Δv + uv² − (F + k)v

The periodic solver enforces each spatial operation together with its prescribed phase shift on the full space–time field. Independent forward integration applies no symmetry projection; it must recover the same phase relations and close the orbit.

THE NUMERICAL SEARCH

A loop has to earn its label.

The bundled waves were found with short-return shooting: evolve through T/2 or T/4, then match the appropriately rotated initial state. A phase condition fixes the time origin, and continuation follows the same branch as parameters change. The browser search also offers a full space–time Crank–Nicolson residual optimizer. Averaging over the exact catalog operations enforces the selected time symmetry, including the affine offsets of g99. Both concentrations and the period are optimized. A motion constraint discourages collapse into a steady state.

A small collocation residual alone is insufficient. A candidate must also pass an independent forward integration, retain spatial structure and motion, and satisfy every group operation. A successful finite-grid result still needs refinement in space and time; it is not a proof of a continuum orbit or of its minimal symmetry group.
