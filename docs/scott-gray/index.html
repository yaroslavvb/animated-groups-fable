What qualifies as verified?

A nonuniform, moving periodic orbit must pass every gate: absolute and relative PDE residuals, exact time symmetry, independent forward return and whole-trajectory agreement, a repeat at half the integration timestep, and checks against collapsed time shifts. Presets and moving transients never enter this filter. These are finite-grid numerical checks, not a continuum existence proof. Tolerances and method →

FIRST: IS IT POSSIBLE?

Six compatible symmetries. Six existence questions.

Gray–Scott is equivariant under square-lattice rotations and translations. Every group here advances time; none reverses it. Their constraints are therefore compatible with the equations. This establishes feasibility, not the existence of a particular glider orbit.

GroupTime actionMost plausible search targetAssessment

A single rigidly drifting glider lattice cannot have a quarter-turn symmetry: its drift velocity would have to equal its own 90° rotation. Look instead for rotating packets, coordinated gliders, or oscillating worms. The mirror-plus-time-shift idea is also compatible with these equations, but mirrors belong to other wallpaper families; 442 itself has rotations only.

THE EQUATIONS

Local chemistry, global timing.

∂u/∂t = Dᵤ Δu − uv² + F(1 − u)
∂v/∂t = Dᵥ Δv + uv² − (F + k)v

Live evolution integrates these equations on a periodic square, starting in the selected group’s zero-time-shift spatial symmetry. Future time shifts are a condition to solve for, not something a forward simulation can impose on demand.

THE NUMERICAL SEARCH

A loop has to earn its label.

The solver minimizes a periodic Crank–Nicolson residual on a full space–time grid. Averaging over the exact catalog operations enforces the selected time symmetry, including the affine offsets of g99. Both concentrations and the period are optimized. A motion constraint discourages collapse into a steady state.

A small collocation residual alone is insufficient. A candidate must also pass an independent forward integration, retain spatial structure and motion, and satisfy every group operation. A successful finite-grid result still needs refinement in space and time; it is not a proof of a continuum orbit or of its minimal symmetry group.
