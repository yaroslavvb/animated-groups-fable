What each diagram symbol does

A polar crystal has one direction, the polar axis c, that no symmetry of the crystal reverses. Stand the axis upright. Every symmetry is then an isometry of the horizontal plane (a shift, a turn, a reflection or a glide reflection) together with a rise along the axis:

(p, z) ↦ (g·p, z + h)g an isometry of the plane, h a height counted modulo the period c

A colour plate is the crystal seen from above, looking down the axis. It shows g as it is. The rise h is carried by the colours, one colour for each height that occurs, so N colours cut the period into N equal steps. (In an entry’s animation the same height is the phase of the loop.) Each mark on a plate stands for one pair (g, h): its shape says what happens in the picture, its decoration how far up.

Nothing turns the axis over (in the animation, nothing runs time backwards), so the marks for operations that would (inversion centres, rotoinversion axes, mirrors and two-fold axes lying across c) never occur on these plates. The 21 marks below are all that do.

Reading a figure

  • Tiles are the copies of one motif. Their colour is their height, as on the plates.
  • In the crystal: one period, with the axis up. The floor is height 0 and the dashed frame is height c, where everything repeats: the paler tiles up there are the bottom ones again. A stem drops from each tile to its footprint on the floor, and the mark lies on the floor where the plate has it.
  • On the plate: the same tiles seen from above, each with its height written beside it as a fraction of c.
  • The dark-outlined tile is carried by the operation from one copy to the next, again and again. Pause it, step it once, or look straight down the axis to watch the crystal turn into its plate.
  • Move every tile applies the operation to the whole crystal at once. It lands on itself, which is what makes the operation a symmetry.
  • Drag the crystal to turn it. Drag the ringed tile on the plate to move the motif: every copy follows.

Translation

(p, z) ↦ (p + t, z)

All marks ↑
In the crystal
A shift by a lattice vector that lies across the axis. Nothing rises: every tile slides onto its neighbour at the same height.
On the plate
The pattern repeats along the arrow, colour for colour.
The mark
An open arrow from one lattice point to the next. Its letter (X, Y or α) names the translation in the entry’s generator table.

P1, Pm

A lattice vector with a vertical part, such as the body-centring vector of an I lattice, shifts the picture and steps the colour as well. The plates give it no mark of its own.

Rotation and screw axes

A turn by 360°/n about a line parallel to the axis, together with a rise of m/n of the period. Seen from above the line is a point, and the core of the mark gives n: a lens, a triangle, a diamond or a hexagon for 2, 3, 4 or 6. With no rise it is a rotation axis n. With a rise it is a screw axis nm, named for its counter-clockwise turn, which rises mc/n.

The clockwise turn about the same axis rises (n − m)c/n: one turn after the other leaves the picture where it was and lifts it by exactly one period. A generator table may quote either turn. A screw’s figure starts with the turn that rises less, and the two buttons under it switch between them.

A rotation axis keeps its own points fixed. A screw axis keeps nothing fixed: drag the motif onto the axis and its copies still stand at different heights.

Mirror and glide planes

A reflection in a plane that contains the axis, followed by a shift inside the plane. Seen from above the plane is a line, and the way the line is drawn says where the shift points. In the formulas x is the distance from the plane, y runs along its line, and b is the repeat of the picture along that line, colours ignored.

A mirror keeps its own points fixed. A glide plane keeps nothing fixed, and the bare mirror image of a tile is not in the crystal.

Mirror plane m

(x, y, z) ↦ (−x, y, z)

All marks ↑
In the crystal
A reflection in a plane that contains the axis, and nothing more. Points of the plane stay where they are.
On the plate
A mirror line: the two sides are mirror images in the same colours.
The mark
A solid line: the plane seen edge-on, with no shift to show.

Glide plane a or b

(x, y, z) ↦ (−x, y + b/2, z)

All marks ↑
In the crystal
The reflection, then a shift of half a repeat along the plane’s line. No rise. Twice is the translation b. The bare mirror image is not part of the crystal: the dashed outline in the figure marks the place it would have.
On the plate
A glide line: copies alternate from side to side like footprints, in one colour.
The mark
A dashed line: the shift lies in the picture. The half arrow, which the plates add to the Tables’ symbol, shows which way the entry’s generator steps.

Diamond glide plane d

(x, y, z) ↦ (−x, y + b/2, z + c/4)

All marks ↑
In the crystal
The reflection, then half a repeat along the line and a quarter of a period up. Twice is b + c/2, a centring vector, so only centred lattices have such planes. Four times is the lattice translation 2b + c.
On the plate
A glide line along which the colours run through a cycle of four.

Dashes and dots with arrowheads. The arrows point along the line in the direction that goes with the rise; taken the other way the same glide descends by c/4. Measured in the conventional centred cell both parts are a quarter of an edge.

Reading a mark at a glance

  • Core. Lens, triangle, diamond, hexagon: a turn of order 2, 3, 4, 6. A line: a plane seen edge-on. An open arrow: a translation.
  • Tails. None: no rise. Otherwise count them: the copies around the mark stand at that many heights, so the turn steps a cycle of that many colours.
  • Sense. Tails pointing counter-clockwise: the stair climbs counter-clockwise, a right-handed screw (31, 41, 61, 62). Pointing clockwise: left-handed (32, 43, 64, 65). With two tails (21, 42, 63) there is no handedness.
  • Line. Solid: a reflection only. Dashed: half a repeat along the line. Dotted: half a period along the axis. Dashes and dots: both. With arrowheads: half a repeat along the line and a quarter of a period up.
  • Fixed points. Only rotation axes and mirrors have them. Screw axes, glide planes and translations move every point.
  • Colour. An operation that rises h moves a cycle of N colours on by Nh/c places.
