Future directions: colour groups and clockwork groups

A clockwork group — a film group whose every symmetry runs time forwards — is a wallpaper group coloured by a circle of phases. Below: the dictionary with worked examples, then a census through six colours that keeps three equivalence problems visibly separate.

The name is meant literally. Drop the time-reversing symmetries and what is left of a film group is a wallpaper group in which every operation advances the film by a fixed fraction of the period: a wallpaper pattern with a clock, and a clock that only turns one way. These are the 68 groups the clockwork orbifold symbols name without a prime clause, and the objects this page compares with the classical colour groups.

Result

The 68 clockwork groups (the forward entries of the film-group catalog) have canonical displayed clock orders loading… for exact orders N = 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6. These are not Wieting's counts of all perfect N-colourings. The honest bridge passes through the regular cyclic colour groups.

1. Colour symmetry in one paragraph

A perfect colouring of a wallpaper pattern assigns each copy of the motif one of N colours so that every symmetry of the uncoloured pattern permutes the colours consistently: following van der Waerden and Burckhardt, a colouring is a homomorphism φ from the wallpaper group Γ onto a transitive group of colour permutations, two colourings being equivalent when the pairs (Γ, φ) are. For two colours the data is just the index-2 subgroup H = ker φ of colour-preserving operations, and the classical count is 46 two-coloured (black–white, dichromatic) plane types, alongside the 17 uncoloured and the 17 grey types in which the swap commutes with everything — 80 Shubnikov classes in all. The 46 split further into 28 whose swaps are point operations and 18 whose swap is an anti-translation (Belov's pb′-type coloured lattices) — the exact two-colour ancestors of the c and r stacking prefixes. The subject is older than it looks: Alexander and Herrmann's eighty two-dimensional layer groups (1929) match the 80 dichromatic classes one-for-one, reflection through the layer plane playing colour swap — precisely the trick film groups play with time.

Two notations coexist. Crystallographers, after Belov and Tarkhova, prime the colour-reversing operations (p4′g′m); The Symmetries of Things (Part II, chapters 11–13) instead names a colouring by the orbifold pair full group / colour-preserving kernel — Escher's threefold reptiles are its 632//333. The clockwork notation's reversal clause is the ToS pair read backwards: o/m′[*×] lists the kernel with its clocks, then the full projection in the bracket — the pair *× / o.

2. Two colours, first way: play it backwards

Time reversal is a two-colouring of spacetime: the forward part G+ sits inside a film group G with index 2, exactly as the colour-preserving subgroup sits inside a dichromatic group. Paint every copy of the motif by its time sign and the film becomes a black–white pattern; "swap the colours" reads "play the film backwards". Restricted to the film groups with no clock at all — every forward operation instantaneous, no time-centred lattice — this correspondence is exact enough to count. The catalog contains … grey classes (the products /1′) and … proper clockless reversal classes. Against the classical 46 the proper count reads 45, and the missing pair is a theorem, not an accident. The one absent type is (cmm, cm) — Belov's cmm′ — and it is not missing from the catalog, only from the clockless census: re-base the spacetime lattice, trading the colour-preserving translation axis for time, and its centred spatial lattice becomes a time-centring. The catalog files it as the clocked class c**/m′[*2222]. This is the reversal-layer instance of the mechanism that files ~*~* under ××; the strict frame-preserving classification (283 classes) separates the two readings again. The forward layer is counted properly in §6 below.

3. Two colours, second way: wait half a period

Colour symmetry does not care whether the swap is realised by an orientation-reversing operation of time or by time itself. A film group whose clock takes only the values 0 and ½ paints the same two colours unitarily: copies at phase 0 in one colour, copies at phase ½ in the other, and the swap is "wait half a period". Time-centred lattices are the purest case — c222121 is the checkerboard whose two sublattices trade colours every half period — and the 2₁ screws and tilded mirrors are the point-operation swaps. The data is once more a pair (Γ, index-2 kernel), so this is a second film reading of the dichromatic types: the catalog has … forward classes whose clock has exact order 2, against the classical 46 — a gap the census in §6 measures and §7 explains.

4. N colours: the clock as a paint pot

Now let the colour group be cyclic of order N. A perfect ℤN-colouring of Γ is a homomorphism φ: Γ → ℤN; a clockwork group whose offsets have denominator N is literally this datum, with φ(g) = N·τ(g) mod N. Sample the film at N equally spaced instants and paint each copy by the instant at which it is, say, full: a time screw nk becomes a gyration that advances the colour by k — Escher's trick of rotating a pattern into its own recolouring. The static colourings below are computed from the film specs themselves (hue = phase): four colours cycling around every 4-centre of 414121, six around every 6-centre of 613121.

5. Which colourings become clocks?

Let Γ be a wallpaper group and H the subgroup preserving one chosen colour. A perfect N-colouring is a transitive coset action on Γ/H, so [Γ:H] = N. It extends directly to one clockwork group precisely when

H ◁ Γ  and  Γ/H ≅ CN,

or equivalently when there is a surjection χ:Γ ↠ CN with kernel H. Normalize a film period to one and embed CN in ℝ/ℤ by ι(j̅) = j/N. The corresponding graph extension is

Gχ = {(γ, t) ∈ Γ × ℝ : t mod ℤ = ι(χ(γ))}.

The colour assigned by χ has become a time offset. Conversely, a forward film with a fixed spatial projection and finite phase image produces such a cyclic character. For N = 2 every index-two subgroup is normal with quotient C2, so the independently derived cyclic census reproduces Wieting's classical 46 exactly.

Projection is not a single frame

The catalog places its 275 canonical representatives into 17 wallpaper projection categories: forget each operation's time component and retain its spatial action. This is not, in general, the symmetry group visible in one frame. For a forward film, the symmetries preserving a generic frame form the zero-phase kernel H, often a proper subgroup of the projected wallpaper group Γ. Reversing groups can also have special-frame stabilizers; accidental motif symmetries are a separate issue.

Wieting counts every transitive action on N colours, while a single clock only supplies a cyclic regular action. Plane colourings and film groups are then quotiented by different affine equivalences. The columns answer related questions, not nested versions of one question.

7. Why the cyclic and film rows differ

The catalog uses the 275-class spacetime equivalence: Galilean boosts and spacetime-lattice re-basings are allowed, and proper three-dimensional orientation is retained. A plane colour class therefore need not remain one film normal form of the same displayed order.

Why five disappears

There are four regular cyclic five-colour plane types, but planar crystallographic point groups have no order-five operation, so their C5 phase is carried by translations. Under the broader film re-basing equivalence, their corresponding classes have canonical representatives in lower-order clock bins. Hence the canonical order-five film bin is zero.

Why one class can split

The wallpaper group p3 has two regular-cyclic three-colour types. Its gyrational kernel becomes the chiral pair 313131 and 323232; its sublattice kernel becomes r33132. Two unoriented colour kernels therefore give three oriented film normal forms.

These examples explain mechanisms, not a canonical subtraction between the aggregate rows. A spacetime re-basing can change the chosen spatial projection and clock order, so this report does not claim a class-by-class correspondence where the two equivalence problems do not supply one.

10. The circle: colouring without quantisation

Set aside the sampling. A clockwork group simply is a homomorphism-like cocycle φ: Γ → ℝ/ℤ — a colouring of the wallpaper group by the circle of phases — classified, as the notation page describes, by H¹(P, ℝ³/L) and its normaliser action. Every clockwork decoration is chromatic data read continuously: gyration subscripts are the colours of rotations, tildes the half-turn of colour on mirrors, the c and r prefixes the colours of translations, and the reversal clause the anti-unitary layer that discrete colour theory calls black–white. The clockwork orbifold symbol is, in this reading, a chromatic orbifold symbol for the colour group ℝ/ℤ — the discrete colour notations are its quotients, and the film is what a colouring looks like when the colours are moments.

11. Directions

Four seem worth walking. (i) Coloured clockwork. A fully coloured clockwork orbifold — The Symmetries of Things' pair notation with clocks on the kernel, or equivalently Belov-style primes distributed over the bracketed projection's features — would unify this catalog with the two-colour tables and settle the residual superscript-letter collisions structurally. (ii) Colour × time. Films of coloured patterns: cocycles into ℤM × ℝ/ℤ, whose classification would organise, e.g., animations that recolour as they loop. (iii) Non-cyclic colour groups. Perfect colourings with colour group S₃ or the quaternion trick of Wieting have no film analogue yet — they would correspond to motifs carrying non-abelian internal states, a step from clocks toward connections. (iv) The chromatic count as homology. The tables above suggest reading the discrepancy between chromatic and film equivalence as the action of the boost/re-basing group on H¹ — a finite computation this repository already performs, worth stating as theorems rather than counts.

12. Scope boundary

This report counts how classical plane colour data is read as the clock displayed by a canonical forward representative. It does not count independent N-colourings of each of the 68 clockwork groups. That would be a new three-dimensional finite-index subgroup census and would first require a choice among parent-group, full affine, and Galilean equivalence. No such larger number is asserted here.
