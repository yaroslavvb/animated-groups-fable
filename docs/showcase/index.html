Showcase

Every field animation, every three-colour colouring, and every black-and-white picture the catalogue holds — on one page, grouped by wallpaper group and running. A card opens the page where its parameters live — the reaction–diffusion family page, the Gyre or Trefoil explorer, a monochrome explorer, or its own standalone viewer — with the film group, the equation, the parameter set and the pattern already selected.

Both two-colour tiers are here: the strict pictures, whose law holds in space and in time, and the broad ones, whose involution exchanges the inks on every frozen frame. Several readings of one wave therefore stand next to each other, which is the point of scrolling — each is a different two-colour pattern of the same film. Only the plain-threshold controls are left out: they carry no two-colour law at all, and they are read beside their own picture in the monochrome explorer, where the page can say so.

Each preview is a short looping clip rendered offline by the same rules the live pages draw with — the ember ramp for a field, the argmax of three samples for a colour entry, the sign of the difference field for a black-and-white one — from the same saved bytes, never captured off a running page. They are reconstructions and not recordings: the CPU twin takes one sample per frame and averages the colours it assigned over a 3× supersampled grid, where the live pages composite a multi-tap temporal shutter, so a fast boundary is a little crisper here than it is there. One card is the exception: the chair tiling has no field to replay, so its card holds that page's own preview image, still.

One clip is drawn per distinct picture, and a picture is carded under every wallpaper group that hosts it — so there are fewer pictures here than cards, and the card's tooltip names the other groups it appears in. Several catalogue records that share a field are likewise one animation under different clockworks, and the card says which — and the record count above counts each record once, however many sections card it. Where several cards of one wave stand together they are not repeats, and the card's last line begins with what makes this one different: the rule a black-and-white picture was read by, the node vector a three-colour one turns about, or — where the catalogue really does say the same thing twice, as it does for six verified colourings of one orbit on six different saved fields — that field's own short digest, the same one its clip is named by. A card that is off the screen is paused and holds no decoder.
