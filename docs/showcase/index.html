Both two-colour tiers are here: the strict pictures, whose law holds in space and in time, and the broad ones, whose involution exchanges the inks on every frozen frame. Many readings of one wave are therefore a run of cards that draw the same picture to the eye — seven verified solutions at F = .00395 … .00405 are seven records and one black diamond around one white blob. Those runs are folded: the clips are measured against each other frame by frame, and where a run of them is one picture a single card is shown carrying a +6 alike chip that opens the rest in place, right behind it. The two promises are the ones you can check by opening a chip — nothing is hidden behind a card that does not look like it, and no two cards a group shows are that close to each other. Nothing is dropped, either: every folded card keeps its own parameters, its own reading and its own link, and Fold look-alikes in the bar below turns the whole thing off. Only the plain-threshold controls are left out: they carry no two-colour law at all, and they are read beside their own picture in the monochrome explorer, where the page can say so.
